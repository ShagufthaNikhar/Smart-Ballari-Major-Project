🏛️ Heritage

The History of Ballari

Ballari's roots stretch back to the Neolithic era, with early settlement evidence found across the district, including nearby Sanganakallu-Kupgal. Over the centuries the region — once known as Kuntala Desha — passed through the hands of the Western Chalukyas, the Hoysalas, and other regional dynasties, leaving behind inscriptions that date the town's recorded history to at least the 12th century.

The city's most celebrated chapter came under the Vijayanagara Empire, whose capital at nearby Hampi (now a UNESCO World Heritage Site) grew into one of the wealthiest cities of its age, trading in diamonds, pearls, horses, and fine silks. Ballari Fort itself, perched atop Ballari Gudda, was built during this era by Hanumappa Nayaka and remains the city's defining landmark today.

In the colonial period Ballari became part of the Ceded Districts under the Madras Presidency, with its municipal council established in 1867. It stayed under Madras (and later Andhra Pradesh administration in parts) until the States Reorganisation of 1956 brought it into Karnataka. The city was formally renamed from "Bellary" to "Ballari" in 2014, and today it's known as a major steel hub of South India.
